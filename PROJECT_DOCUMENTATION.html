1. Executive Summary & Challenge Alignment

EarthTrend Explorer is a scientifically grounded climate intelligence platform and empirical statistical laboratory. Utilizing 43 years (1981–2023, 516 contiguous monthly observations per variable, validated for completeness and missing-value handling) of authentic NASA Earth observation data (NASA POWER / MERRA-2) across three representative Egyptian climate zones—Greater Cairo (urban valley), Alexandria (Mediterranean coast), and Aswan (hyper-arid desert)—the platform investigates long-term environmental shifts, abrupt hydrologic changes, and predictive climate anomalies.

Addressing the NASA Challenge: The platform rigorously answers the four foundational questions posed by the challenge:

  • 1. What is changing? Mean temperatures (T2M), absolute monthly extreme peak hours (T2M_MAX, T2M_MIN), physical precipitation totals (PRECTOTCORR in mm/month and mm/year), multi-scalar drought indices (SPI-3/6/12), and compound hot-dry extremes.
  • 2. Where is it changing? Evaluated across three distinct Egyptian regional regimes mapped to native NASA MERRA-2 0.5° × 0.625° reanalysis grid cells.
  • 3. How much is it changing? Quantified using the robust non-parametric Theil-Sen median slope estimator with Gilbert (1987) 95% confidence intervals, decadal rates, and change-point magnitudes.
  • 4. Is it statistically significant? Evaluated via the non-parametric Mann-Kendall test, Hamed-Rao (1998) serial autocorrelation corrections, Hirsch-Slack Seasonal Mann-Kendall tests, Benjamini-Hochberg FDR (q < 0.05) and Holm-Bonferroni FWER (α < 0.05) multiple testing controls, and the Pettitt (1979) change-point test.

Why It Matters: Regional climate adaptation in vulnerable, water-stressed arid environments requires transparent, physically guarded statistical tools rather than unverified black-box claims. By identifying steady warming (+0.26 to +0.43 °C/decade), an abrupt 2015 rainfall step-change (+181% to +266%), and surging compound hot-dry extremes, EarthTrend Explorer converts authentic NASA observations into reliable, localized climate intelligence for water resource planning and regional resilience.

2. Project Details: Architecture, Features, & Status

Operational Architecture & Methodology

EarthTrend Explorer functions as a unified pipeline structured across four analytical modules:

  1. Data Ingestion & Physical Conversion: Ingests monthly air temperature (T2M), absolute monthly extreme peak hours (T2M_MAX, T2M_MIN), and bias-corrected precipitation flux (PRECTOTCORR), converting daily rainfall rates (mm/day) into physical monthly accumulation (mm/month) with leap-year adjustments.
  2. Non-Parametric Trend Engine: Assesses long-term trends without assuming normality. Accounts for rank autocorrelation using the Hamed-Rao modified variance $\text{Var}^*(S)$, uncovering that lag-12 autocorrelation in Alexandria ($\rho_{12} = -0.301$) deflates variance and raises $Z^*$ from +5.50 to +6.23.
  3. Anomaly & Regime Shift Detection: Computes WMO 1981–2010 monthly climatological normal departures ($\mu_m, \sigma_m$), tracks calendar-continuous persistent heat/cold spells ($\ge 3$ months, $|Z| \ge 1.0\sigma$), applies Pettitt change-point detection to identify hydrologic regime breaks, and fits 2-parameter Gamma distributions for Standardized Precipitation Index (SPI) drought monitoring.
  4. Predictive Anomaly Forecasting: Implements three candidate time-series models (Seasonal Naive baseline, Holt-Winters Additive Exponential Smoothing, and SARIMAX). Automatically selects the tournament winner via expanding-window chronological cross-validation across 5 historical origins (2018–2022) using Mean Absolute Scaled Error (MASE). Computes 80% and 95% prediction intervals and evaluates forward projections against the WMO 1981–2010 baseline.

Feature Status, Limitations, & Future Roadmap

Feature Component Status Implementation Details Scientific Consideration / Limitation
Non-Parametric Trends Working Feature Mann-Kendall, Theil-Sen 95% CIs, Hamed-Rao autocorrelation, Seasonal MK, FDR/FWER across 12 months. Statistical trends document observational changes; they do not single-handedly establish causal attribution.
Physical Rainfall Totals Working Feature Converted daily rate (mm/day) to physical monthly/annual accumulated totals with leap-year accuracy. Reanalysis grid cells represent area averages, which smooth out local convective cloudbursts.
Pettitt Change-Point Test Working Feature Detected abrupt step-change in rainfall between 2014 and 2015 in Cairo ($p=0.0067$) and Alexandria ($p=0.0060$). Detects when a statistical break occurred, not why. Requires ground-truth validation against EMA gauges.
SPI Drought & Aridity Guardrails Working Feature Multi-scale SPI (3, 6, 12) via Gamma MLE. Flags SPI as low-reliability when baseline zero frequency $q \ge 0.50$. In hyper-arid regions (Aswan, zero rain in 86% of months), SPI is mathematically bounded above -1.0σ ($\Phi^{-1}(q)$).
Forecasting Tournament Working Feature 5-origin expanding window backtesting (2018–2022); evaluated by MAE, RMSE, and MASE. Winner: Holt-Winters ETS. Statistical extrapolations are probabilistic projections with uncertainty, not deterministic future guarantees.
2024 Reality Check Working Feature Forecasts made from 1981–2023 are scored against real 2024 NASA observations strictly held out from training. Tests out-of-sample skill and evaluates binomial calibration of 80% and 95% prediction intervals.
GPM IMERG Cross-Validation Future Roadmap Cross-validate the 2015 MERRA-2 rainfall jump against NASA GPM IMERG satellite constellation observations. Will distinguish physical climatic shifts from potential reanalysis observing-system sensor transitions.
Spatial NetCDF Grid Ingestion Future Roadmap Direct ingestion of spatial bounding box NetCDF cubes across Egypt via xarray. Current version uses verified NASA POWER point extractions at representative coordinates.
